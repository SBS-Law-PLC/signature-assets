SBS Law, PLC — Email Signature Kit

Firm-wide Gmail signature — August 5, 2026. Find your name, copy your block, paste it into Gmail. No edits needed — every block is finished.

How to install in Gmail

  1. Open this page in Chrome, find your name below, and select the entire signature inside the Light box — click just above the logo and drag to the end of the confidentiality notice.
  2. Ctrl+C to copy. Copy from the rendered page, not from source — that is what carries the formatting and the logo.
  3. In Gmail: Settings (gear) → See all settings → General → scroll to Signature.
  4. Create new (or select your existing one and clear it), then Ctrl+V into the box.
  5. Under Signature defaults, set it for both NEW EMAILS USE and ON REPLY/FORWARD USE. Leave the "insert this signature before quoted text" box unchecked.
  6. Save Changes at the bottom. Send one test message to yourself and check it on your phone too.
If the logo does not come through the paste: place your cursor at the top of the signature box, click the Insert image button in that toolbar, upload sbs-logo-horizontal-fullcolor-322x78.png (sent alongside this page), then click the image once and choose Small. Gmail sometimes drops pasted images depending on the browser.

Spacing below the message body

What the mailbox audit found

Every current signature in the firm was pulled and compared. There was no house style — there were four, plus two people with nothing. This is what the template resolves.

  1. Two different confidentiality notices were in circulation. Kevin, Rawan, and Whisper sent the long ECPA notice; Todd and Hailey sent a shorter one; six of eleven sent none at all. Reverted 11 Aug 2026 to the long ECPA notice — the text the firm has been publishing all along. Verified word-for-word against two live signatures sent that morning (Kevin's and the Administrator's); the kit now matches what clients receive.
  2. One notice named the wrong firm. Whisper's said Sutherland Law PLC. Fixed.
  3. Two people had no signature. Mike Harris and Jason Stoops. Both now have blocks.
  4. Signatures fired inconsistently. Sherry's appeared on nearly every message; Rawan's on roughly one in five, Olivia's on two in five — both had sent client-facing mail with none. Setting the signature as the Gmail default fixes this.
  5. Four typographic styles. Georgia blue (John, Hailey, Olivia), Arial (Todd), Verdana grey (Samantha), and Gmail default (Kevin, Sherry, Whisper, Rawan). Now one.
  6. Address written four ways — with and without the "N.", with and without commas. Now one.
  7. Titles were non-parallel — Paralegal, Chief Paralegal, Paralegal/Legal Admin. Kept as each person uses them, but set in one style.
  8. Badges now have a defined slot with alt text and dimensions set. Kevin carried two images and John one, none with alt text and one with no size set at all. The badge moves to a fixed 110×92 column on the right — measured at the same block height with a badge as without — and every image now carries width, height, and alt attributes so blocked images render as a labeled placeholder rather than a broken-image icon.
  9. Todd's block had no phone in the contact area — his only number was buried in the disclaimer. Now on every block.

Decisions behind this version

  1. Where the details came from. The address, main line, and confidentiality notice were lifted from the Managing Partner's live signature, not composed here — so what goes out matches what clients already receive.
  2. No bar numbers. Dropped firm-wide. Licensure varies across states and districts, and only one attorney was carrying a P-number anyway. Anyone who wants theirs can type it after their name; nothing in the block depends on it.
  3. Titles. Sherry Trujillo is Chief Paralegal, per her own signature.
